## What This Repository Is

This repository contains long-form walkthrough documents that derive specific results in physics from the Event Density (ED) framework's substrate primitives. Each walkthrough is self-contained — readable and verifiable without external materials. The reader brings standard physics and mathematics background; the walkthrough supplies the framework's substrate ontology, the load-bearing arguments, and the chain from primitives to result.

The walkthroughs complement the main Event Density repository at https://github.com/allen-proxmire/event-density, which contains the framework's source papers, theorems, arc memos, and supporting work. The walkthroughs here are exposition documents — they present the framework's results to readers who want to follow a complete derivation in one document rather than navigating cross-references across the source corpus.

Each walkthrough is structured similarly: a framing of the question, the substrate ontology, the load-bearing structural moves, the result, and an honest accounting of what the argument establishes versus what remains open or inherited. The methodological discipline is consistent throughout — derive what the substrate primitives can deliver, identify what's structurally forced versus value-inherited, and refuse to overclaim.

## What Event Density Is

Event Density is a substrate ontology framework. Reality consists of discrete acts of becoming (micro-events) connected by persistent rules (chains as worldlines), communicating through adjacency-mediated structure (channels), with local participation density (bandwidth) carrying the framework's quantitative content. The framework's primitive stack — discreteness, finite participation bandwidth, commitment irreversibility, individuation, polarity, four-band bandwidth decomposition, rule-type taxonomy, relational timing, and others — produces, as forced theorems, foundational structures across multiple domains of physics.

The framework's methodological signature is the form-FORCED / value-INHERITED distinction. Functional forms of physical laws follow as theorems from substrate commitments. Specific numerical parameters (ℏ, c, particle masses, gauge couplings, kernel widths) are inherited from material-specific physics or experimental input rather than predicted by the framework. This discipline is preserved at every layer.

The framework's reach is bounded but specific. It produces structural content in nominally separate physics domains — non-relativistic and relativistic quantum mechanics, gravitational dynamics including MOND and the BTFR, black hole architecture, mass content, spin-statistics, fluid mechanics, gauge theory — through the same substrate primitives applied with different structural moves. Where the framework cannot deliver (continuous numerical content, mass ratios, quantitative resolution of Clay-prize problems), it says so explicitly.

The framework is being developed publicly by independent physicist Allen Proxmire. The full technical program is at https://github.com/allen-proxmire/event-density. This walkthroughs repository hosts the exposition documents.

## How to Read These

Each walkthrough is independently readable. You can pick any one and follow it without having read the others.

If you want a sense of the framework's range and methodological pattern before picking one, the order below moves from foundational quantum mechanics through gravity, mass, statistics, and Clay-prize-relevance territory. Reading three or four in sequence gives a coherent picture.

The walkthroughs assume mathematical and physics background appropriate to graduate-level study — quantum mechanics, special relativity, group theory, partial differential equations, Hilbert space methods. Specialized topics (Cl(3,1) Clifford algebras for Dirac, Lyapunov methods for Navier-Stokes, Osterwalder-Schrader axioms for Yang-Mills) are introduced in context within their respective walkthroughs.

Each walkthrough names what's forced, what's inherited, what's open, and what's refuted. Read those sections carefully. The framework's discipline is what makes the results meaningful — overclaiming would dilute everything.

## The Methodological Pattern

Across these walkthroughs, a consistent methodological pattern emerges. The framework's substrate primitives produce structural content cleanly when the question is about classifications, dichotomies, structural form, or existence claims. The framework's primitives do not produce continuous numerical relationships between rule-types or quantitative resolutions of value-inherited competitions.

This pattern is visible in the verdicts:

Walkthroughs 1-9 reach **strong positive closures** because their results are about structural form (Born rule, Schrödinger, Klein-Gordon, Dirac), structural existence (gravity, black holes), or structural classifications (Bell-Tsirelson bound, Heisenberg uncertainty, arrow of time). The substrate primitives can deliver these.

Walkthrough 10 (mass) reaches an **H1-dominant closure** because mass values, ratios, and hierarchies are continuous numerical content outside the primitive stack's reach. The framework fixes the structure of mass — the σ_τ form, the massless slot existence, the Fierz-class type classification — but not its values. The negative result is itself structurally informative.

Walkthrough 11 (spin-statistics) reaches a **strong positive closure** because it ties two ℤ_2 dichotomies (exchange phase and integer/half-integer spin) into a single forced equality. The substrate primitives plus 3+1D topology plus Cl(3,1) algebraic uniqueness handle this cleanly — the inputs are the right kind for the structural machinery.

Walkthroughs 12 (NS smoothness) and 13 (YM existence and mass gap) reach **parallel Intermediate Path C verdicts**. Each Clay problem decomposes into substrate-derived canonical-ED content (R1 for NS; full canonical dynamics with mass-gap mechanism for YM) plus a value-inherited condition that determines quantitative resolution (R1 versus super-Burnett quantitative competition for NS; kernel-profile rescaling exponent for YM). Neither walkthrough claims a Clay-problem solution. Both identify the load-bearing inherited conditions explicitly.

The methodological discipline that makes these results meaningful is the willingness to refuse to overclaim. Most theoretical frameworks that touch foundational physics either claim broad applicability without confronting limits or focus narrowly on specific phenomena without engaging the substrate question. The framework engages substrate-level structural questions and is honest about where its reach ends. The form-forced / value-inherited distinction is not a hedge — it is a clear-eyed account of what the substrate primitives can and cannot deliver.

## Verifying the Walkthroughs

These walkthroughs are intended to be verifiable. Each one presents the substrate ontology, the load-bearing arguments, and the chain from primitives to result in enough detail that a reader with appropriate background can follow each move and check whether it goes through.

The verification questions a reader should ask, walkthrough by walkthrough:

Are the substrate primitives clearly stated?

Are the load-bearing structural moves explicit, with each step's justification named?

Are the alternatives that get refuted (where applicable) explicitly considered and refuted with substantive arguments rather than hand-waved away?

Are forced content, inherited content, and open questions clearly demarcated at the end?

Does the result actually follow from the chain of arguments presented, or does it require additional inputs that aren't named?

Where the framework reaches a partial closure (Arc M H1-dominant, NS Intermediate Path C, YM Intermediate Path C), is the partial nature of the closure honestly framed?

Each walkthrough is structured to make these checks possible. If a verification failure is found — an argument that doesn't go through, an alternative that wasn't considered, an inherited input that wasn't named — that's substantive feedback worth raising.

## A Note on the Framework's Status

Event Density is an open research framework. The walkthroughs document closed structural content — results that have been derived through substrate primitives and verified within the framework's existing technical apparatus. Open territories remain. Future walkthroughs may close the Hawking spectrum derivation (currently open in the BH arc), extend the NS analysis toward Path C+ quantitative resolution (requiring research-program-level work outside the canon), or consolidate the framework's gauge-theoretic content via the planned ED-QFT unified overview paper.

The framework is not a finished theory. Whether the substrate commitments are correct is the load-bearing empirical question, and the walkthroughs document the framework's current structural reach without prejudice on that question. Each walkthrough's "what this argument establishes" section names the load-bearing question that each result depends on — discreteness, finite participation bandwidth, commitment irreversibility, the four-band bandwidth decomposition, the rule-type taxonomy, and so forth. The framework stands or falls on whether these are the correct foundational concepts.

The walkthroughs collected here represent the framework's strongest exposition of its current structural content. They are offered for review, verification, criticism, and building-on by anyone interested in the substrate-level foundations of physics.
